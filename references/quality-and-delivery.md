# Quality and delivery

## Improve the image without inventing detail

Probe each source instead of applying one preset blindly. Distinguish true source resolution, bit depth, frame rate, color primaries and transfer function. HLG/HDR footage needs an appropriate SDR viewing transform for SDR delivery; already-SDR clips must not receive the HDR transform again. Set and verify encoded color metadata as well as the pixels. An apparently correct image can still carry incorrect HDR tags.

Use restrained exposure, color/contrast and sharpening adjustments, checking faces, uniforms, court lines and motion for halos or crushed detail. Denoising, stabilization and AI enhancement are optional responses to observed problems, not assumed steps. External processing or paid services require applicable authorization.

Explain that a 4K export from 1080p footage is an upscale, not recovered native detail. Compare the same passage, crop and treatment when deciding between exports. Prefer a clean high-quality source-matched master when upscaling adds no visible benefit. Preserve narrower/square footage without stretching or cutting away the play; retain existing attribution. Do not let aspect-ratio consistency override action visibility.

## Audio and timing

An audio stream may be digitally silent. Measure it before assuming usable court/field sound exists. Preserve genuine sound where available; do not fabricate ambience to imply it was recorded with silent footage. Describe silent source sections when relevant to delivery. Check final encoded loudness and true peaks, not just intermediate files.

Account for freezes, intro/outro cards and frame rounding in the output timeline. Reuse compatible unchanged rendered segments when that avoids unnecessary generation loss; verify codec, frame rate, time base and audio layout before stream-copy concatenation. Keep editable source-linked decisions for future changes.

## Deliver a usable file

Keep a full-quality master separate from smaller viewing copies. Local paths may work on the editing Mac but not on mobile; use supported inline media or a user-authorized delivery route suited to the device. Confirm that a link is usable rather than assuming a private storage preview is shared.

For requested email attachments, inspect the actual service limit and file size. If needed, create and verify a smaller MP4, clearly labeling its resolution/compression and preserving the master. A compressed attachment is not the full-quality export. Use connector file-upload/attachment support when available; avoid exposing binary/base64 data in user-facing outputs.

Respect conditional requests: “send when completed” authorizes delivery upon completion, not automatically sending an unfinished review copy. Check readiness against the agreed scope and QC status before sending. If a review copy is requested, label outstanding checks honestly. Do not claim an email was sent until the service confirms success; record the message ID and delivered version in project notes to avoid duplicates.

If a check cannot be performed with available tools, state the exact limitation and provide the review artifact. Do not keep promising a later automatic check or notification unless a supported follow-up mechanism has actually been configured. Do not call a cut final merely because it rendered or reached the target runtime.
